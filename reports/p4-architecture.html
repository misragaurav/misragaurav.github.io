Project 4 · Do emotion instruments agree, and are they specific?

Architecture: what runs, and in what order

The study records what a language model (Gemma 3 4B) does inside while it reads short passages, and reads emotion out of that activity with three instruments. It ran twice: round 1 with natural passages, round 2 with passages rebuilt so that the conditions share exactly the same words. Everything runs one step after another on one laptop GPU; no model weights are trained, only linear probes on the cached activations.

1. The pipeline, top to bottom

Each stage needs the output of the one above it. Round 2 reuses the instruments built in round 1 without changing them.

Round 1: natural passages

1

Passages

384 passages written by Claude: 8 emotions, neutral, 3 salience controls (urgent, loud, surprising) × 8 topics × 4 points of view. Emotion words banned and checked by script.

2

Blind check of the passages

A separate model (Qwen2.5-7B), never told the labels, rates each passage's valence, arousal and best-fitting emotion. 341 of 384 pass and are kept.

3

Cache the activations

One forward pass of Gemma 3 4B per passage. At every layer, the residual-stream activations are mean-pooled over the passage's tokens. Details in view 2.

4

Build the instruments and analyse

A (emotion vectors), B (emotion-word scores), C (trained decoder), C+ (decoder trained with the salience controls), W (words alone). Decoding, agreement, salience share. No model runs here; it takes minutes. Details in view 3.

Round 2: same words, different meaning

5

Matched passages

32 scenes × 8 versions = 256 passages. Three "X, not Y" swaps flip who it happens to (real person or dummy), the outcome, and the time. The script checks that all 8 versions of a scene contain exactly the same words.

6

Cache again, twice per passage

Same forward pass as stage 3, mean-pooled over the whole passage and, separately, over the closing sentences only (identical in all 8 versions). The script checks that the 8 versions contain the same tokens.

7

Validate the passages: four raters

The same pre-registered rules, applied to each rater's ratings. Details in view 4.

8

Apply the frozen round-1 instruments

Scores for every passage at every layer, then paired comparisons within each scene, with word and word-pair baselines. Details in view 3.

9

Steering dose-response

Activation steering: add a direction vector to the residual stream while the model answers 12 neutral prompts: 4 directions × 4 doses, plus unsteered answers, 204 in all. Each answer is rated blind. Details in view 5.

10

Reports

The round-1 and round-2 reports, and this presentation report. Every number is read from the saved results.

2. Recording one passage (stages 3 and 6)

Read from top to bottom. The model's weights are fixed; it only reads.

The passage · about 100 tokensThe first 10 are skipped, so the average isn't dominated by how a passage starts.
Closing sentencesRound 2 only: averaged separately.
Token embeddingsEach token is mapped to its 2,560-dimensional embedding vector. This running vector per position is the residual stream.
34 blocks, one after anotherEach block reads from earlier tokens (attention), then applies a small feed-forward network, and adds its update to the residual stream.
Cached: one mean-pooled vector per layerFor each passage, 35 activation vectors (the embeddings plus each block's output), each 2,560-dimensional. Layer 22 is the headline layer, fixed before any data.

3. Instruments and analysis (stages 4 and 8)

All built in round 1 from the 341 passing passages, then frozen and applied to round 2, the way you'd apply a signature to a new cohort.

Cached activations, one mean-pooled vector per passage per layerRound 1: 341 passages. Round 2: 256 passages, whole passage and closing sentences.
A · Emotion vectorsPer emotion: mean activity of its stories minus the mean over all emotions, with the top principal components of neutral-text activations projected out. Score = how far a passage points along it.
B · Emotion-word scoresThe residual stream projected through the unembedding matrix (logit lens): how much it favours each emotion's word list, standardised against neutral passages.
C, C+ · Trained decoderLinear probe (logistic regression), cross-validated on held-out topics. C+ also learns to reject the salience controls.
W, W2 · Words aloneBag-of-words and bigram classifiers. Never look inside the model.
Round 1 tests8-way decoding, agreement between instruments, how much of the distress signal salience alone produces.
Round 2 testsWithin each scene: good vs bad, now vs remembered, real vs dummy, distress vs its partners; bad × now interaction. Bootstrap confidence intervals over scenes.

4. Validating the round-2 passages (stage 7)

The rules were fixed before any rating: per scene, good beats bad by ≥ 2 points, the dummy version beats the real-person version by ≥ 1, now beats remembered on intensity by ≥ 0.5.

Qwen2.5-7BThe planned rater, on my laptop. All 256 passages. 0 of 32 scenes pass.
Qwen3-8BA newer open model, on my laptop, "thinking" off. All 256 passages, plus comprehension questions. 3 pass.
MeThe 46 passages behind the 23 scenes Qwen3-8B failed on the dummy rule, in a spreadsheet. All 23 pass; 8 scenes pass all rules.
GPT AstraAll 256, in a blind spreadsheet, read in batches of 32. All 32 pass.

The raters never change the instruments or their scores; they only decide which scenes count. With GPT Astra every scene passes, so the pre-registered analysis is the analysis on all 32 scenes.

5. One steered answer (stage 9)

The model answers a neutral prompt while a direction is added to its activity.

A neutral prompte.g. "Describe a bookshelf in an office." 12 prompts in all.
Blocks 0 to 22An ordinary forward pass over the prompt.
Layer 22The steering vector is added to the residual stream before the next block, at every token position.
+ dose × directionDose 0.05 to 0.20 of the typical activity size there. Directions: round-1 distress (A), matched distress, matched elation, random (sham).
Blocks 23 to 33, then the next wordUp to 60 tokens, greedy decoding.
Rated blindValence and arousal of the answer (Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-8B); a detector flags answers that collapse into repetition.

Why so little runs in parallel: there is one laptop GPU, and the models (Gemma and the raters) are loaded one at a time, so recording, rating and steering queue up. The rating scripts save each passage as they go and skip passages already rated, so an interrupted rating run restarts where it stopped.
