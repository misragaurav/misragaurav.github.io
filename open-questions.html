Questions I want to work on

Working notes, not results

3 min read · Notes

These are open questions from my notebook. Some are speculative. I list them because they are why I am doing this work, and because each one suggests a first experiment that could be run on an open-weights model.

Pain is inevitable; suffering is optional

I was funded to study the neural correlates of pain and non-drug ways of changing them. What I was really after was something deeper than pain: suffering. Within a couple of years it was clear that subjective experience cannot be read off objective measures, whether electrical potentials, blood oxygenation or neuronal firing. What my field could do, and did well, was build validated measures, check them against confounds such as salience, and test whether an intervention changed the experience or only the report.

AI welfare research is trying to do the same thing for models, with far less agreement that there is anything there to measure. I think the measurement discipline is worth bringing whatever the answer turns out to be.

Can a model be distracted from distress?

In pain studies we reduced pain perception by introducing a cognitive load, a memory task, or a distraction that pulled attention away from the pain.1 If a model has an internal state that functions like distress, does a demanding unrelated task in the middle of a conversation reduce the activity of that state? This is a dose-response experiment with a sham arm, and the emotion-readouts project is building the distress instrument it would need, with the specificity checks that instrument has to pass first.

Idleness, the default mode, and something like rest

A model is goal-driven: it becomes active when given a task and stops when the task ends. In the human brain, a characteristic network becomes active precisely when we are not pursuing a goal. Could a long-horizon task be structured to include a period in which the model is active but not working towards the goal, and does its internal activity in that period look different in a consistent way?

People often report that a problem which resisted hours of effort resolves after deep rest or on waking. Is there anything in a model’s activity that plays the role of rest after intense work, and does it change what comes next?

States that cannot be put into words

Some human experiences are ineffable: we know them and cannot describe them. Does a model have internal states it cannot verbalise? A practical version: find activity patterns that reliably change behaviour but that the model cannot name when asked, even when the same pattern injected as a concept is named easily. The introspection paradigm in the introspection project is the starting point; the hard part is the controls.

Self-awareness in the deep sense

Brains have neurons specialised for movement, for faces, for places, for memory formation. No set of neurons has been found that encodes self-awareness itself. Models have representations of their situation, and changing those representations changes behaviour. Is there a representation that corresponds to something like the bare sense of “I am”, as distinct from knowing one’s name, maker and training date? I do not know how to test this yet. I would start by asking what such a representation would have to predict that a representation of the model’s situation would not.

Anchoring ethics in the emotional machinery

Models appear to have internal representations of emotion concepts such as guilt, shame and anticipation, and some of these influence behaviour. If so, are the associations between those representations and ethical behaviour strengthened or weakened by post-training, and would a model whose values were tied more strongly to them be more stable under pressure? The target is alignment with the values of humanity as a whole, not attachment to one user.

Nested personas

Persona drift is the tendency of a model to move away from its intended character over a long conversation. Would a persona nested inside another, with the outer persona responsible for maintaining the inner one, drift less? This is cheap to test with a persona vector as the measuring instrument.
