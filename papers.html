Pain: perception, measurement and modulation

  • Automated classification of pain perception using high-density electroencephalography data. Misra, G., Wang, W., Archer, D. B., Roy, A., & Coombes, S. A. Journal of Neurophysiology, 117(2), 786–795 (2017). Full paper
    A machine-learning decoder of high versus low pain perception from 128-channel EEG. The ancestor of the probes and specificity tests in my language-model work.
  • Pain-related suppression of beta oscillations facilitates voluntary movement. Misra, G., Ofori, E., Chung, J. W., & Coombes, S. A. Cerebral Cortex, 27(4), 2592–2606 (2017). Full paper
    Acute pain suppressed specific EEG frequency bands and shortened reaction time, without changing muscle activity at the site of pain or the accuracy of the movement.
  • Neuroimaging evidence of motor control and pain processing in the human midcingulate cortex. Misra, G., & Coombes, S. A. Cerebral Cortex, 25(7), 1906–1919 (2015). Full paper
    fMRI contrasts showing where pain and motor control overlap in the midcingulate cortex. See contrast maps.
  • Pain and motor processing in the human cerebellum. Coombes, S. A., & Misra, G. Pain, 157(1), 117–127 (2016). Full paper
  • Dose-response effect of isometric force production on the perception of pain. Misra, G., Paris, T. A., Archer, D. B., & Coombes, S. A. PLoS ONE, 9(2), e88105 (2014). Full paper · open access
    A fixed noxious stimulus and a graded motor intervention. See dose-response.
  • Effects of a force production task and a working memory task on pain perception. Paris, T. A., Misra, G., Archer, D. B., & Coombes, S. A. The Journal of Pain, 14(11), 1492–1501 (2013). Full paper
