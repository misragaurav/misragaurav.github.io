Concepts

The methods behind the research pages, explained for readers from either side: what each idea is, how it is used in pain and cognitive neuroscience, and how it applies to a language model.

  • Signal detection theoryPsychophysics3 min read

    Every yes/no report mixes two things, sensitivity and bias. Signal detection theory separates them, which is why it is the first tool to reach for when a model says it notices something.

  • Dose-response and the psychometric functionPsychophysics3 min read

    Vary the intensity of a stimulus or an intervention in steps and plot the response. Thresholds, slopes and ceilings each answer a different question, and a curve can tell sensitivity from bias where a single point cannot.

  • Contrast maps and the subtraction methodNeuroimaging2 min read

    Brain maps and concept vectors are both built by subtraction, condition minus control. Choosing the control is choosing what the map means, and a careless control can manufacture a finding.

  • Salience, and why it haunts EEG researchEEG3 min read

    Much of the brain's response to a painful stimulus is also produced by any sudden, attention-grabbing event. Salience controls are how pain research learned to tell the two apart, and how a distress probe in a language model should be tested.

  • Validity, specificity, and the multitrait-multimethod testMeasurement2 min read

    An instrument should agree with other instruments for the same trait, disagree with instruments for different traits, and not respond to things that merely travel with the trait. Psychology has tested this since 1959; interpretability probes rarely are.
