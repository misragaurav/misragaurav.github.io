About

I am a measurement scientist of subjective states. For four years I measured pain in people, with EEG, fMRI and EMG. Now I apply the same methods, signal detection, dose-response, contrast design, decoding and specificity testing, to the internal states of language models.

The path here

  • Engineering and biophysics. A BTech in chemical engineering at IIT Bombay, a PhD at the University of Florida on multi-scale modelling of semi-flexible filaments, and a postdoc in bioengineering at Stanford on the mechanics of bacterial cell walls. Physical models of soft, living matter.
  • Pain and motor neuroscience, 2012 to 2016. Research scientist in the Laboratory for Rehabilitation Neuroscience at the University of Florida. I designed and analysed studies of how pain and movement interact, with high-density EEG, fMRI and EMG: an EEG decoder of pain perception, the finding that acute pain suppresses beta oscillations and speeds movement, and the overlap of pain and motor control in the midcingulate cortex. See publications.
  • Founding and building, 2016 to 2025. Principal investigator on a National Science Foundation SBIR grant for context-aware health interventions delivered through phones and watches (Quantified Habits). Founding data scientist at a data-strategy consultancy, where machine-learning work for a biotech start-up’s bacterial-identification hardware contributed to its acquisition. VP of data strategy at an AI marketing platform. Co-founder and CTO of SE.ai, building agentic language-model workflows for FDA 510(k) regulatory submissions.
  • Now. Independent AI and machine-learning consultant. My main client is a rehabilitation neuroscience lab at Colorado State University, where I do machine-learning research (a 2026 Scientific Reports paper on predicting motor learning after stroke, two more under review), built a screening layer that checks every input to the lab’s AI agent for hidden instructions, and teach graduate students and postdocs to use AI safely.

Why language models

Until 2025 I thought of these systems as sophisticated next-word predictors with no understanding of the world. Watching what they can now do changed my mind, and it reopened a question I had set aside: whether subjective states can be measured at all from the outside. In people I learned that you cannot read experience off a scan, but you can build measures, test them against confounds, and check whether an intervention changes the state or only the report. Language models offer complete access to the system and no agreement about whether there is anything to report. That combination needs exactly the discipline my field learned the hard way.

The research pages show what that looks like in practice, including an experiment that did not replicate and what it taught me. The notes are where I keep the questions I most want to work on.

Outside work

A yoga and meditation practitioner for over 25 years and a certified yoga instructor. Meditation is, among other things, long practice in observing one’s own states. One of my favourite side projects was predicting the onset of my wife’s anger from a wearable EEG headset, with wavelet analysis.
